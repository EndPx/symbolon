## Active target: BitSafe Contribution Pool

The selected target is the **20,000 CC Contribution Pool**, for a reproducible LocalNet demo. This path does not require us to operate a Canton node. The technical starter is the [BitSafe hackathon LocalNet](https://github.com/DLC-link/decentralization-manager/tree/hackathon/hackathon): its pinned setup creates three local Canton participants and three DecMan nodes, and its demo exercises a 2-of-3 decentralized party. The [starter README](https://github.com/DLC-link/decentralization-manager/blob/hackathon/hackathon/README.md) requires 12 GB RAM and 4 CPUs allocated to Docker, plus about 20 GB free disk. Its LocalNet has auth disabled and is for a local demo, not a DevNet deployment.

Symbolon's repo lifecycle demo runs on a separate local Canton sandbox. The [BitSafe adapter](../guides/bitsafe-localnet.md) now defines a `PriceMarkProposal` implementing `GovernableAction`. A local Daml Script has verified that one confirmation cannot execute the action, while two of three confirmations publish a new Symbolon mark and enable a dealer margin call on an active repo. The script also passed twice on a live single-participant Canton sandbox. On 5 October 2026 it also passed through the official three DecMan services: governed initialization and mark update, rejection below threshold, repo margin handling and repayment, with matching execution update IDs across all participants. See the [verified evidence](../reference/status.md). The starter's generic vote alone has no Symbolon effect. Threshold control also does not prove the price is economically correct. Confirm the expected Contribution Pool integration depth with a BitSafe mentor.

Our judge-facing proof plan is to show a clean setup from pinned versions, the decentralized-party topology and threshold, the Symbolon state before and after authorization, a below-threshold rejection and a successful threshold execution through DecMan, plus a complete repo result in the app or ledger record. Add a short recording and exact commands so a reviewer can reproduce it. Confirm the required integration depth with BitSafe; the LocalNet integration has been reproduced, while Contribution Pool eligibility and award decisions remain with BitSafe.

## Gold route (deferred)

Organizer messages supplied to the team from 21–22 September 2026 distinguish independent node work from placing several parties on a single shared participant. The guidance says Gold requires access to an own node; a party may be hosted across multiple nodes, including Noders/BitSafe infrastructure, and DevNet is the minimum environment. Several isolated parties on one shared participant do not establish the requested multi-node setup.

The Gold prize is **30,000 CC** for a live Decentralized Party on DevNet or MainNet integrated into a working application. Teams must apply for the Gold deployment path by **4 October 2026**; that is the application deadline, not the final deployment deadline. Deployment evidence is due with the **9 October submission**. The shared hackathon DevNet is not configured to host decentralized parties for every team. A VPS is only a potential host: the team still needs a suitable Canton participant node, access to its Admin and Ledger APIs, Decentralization Manager, and independent peer hosting.

Gold is not the current submission target. If it is reopened later, the path is to verify VPS and DevNet participant capacity; apply and review architecture with BitSafe; deploy an own participant and DecMan; form a Decentralized Party with independent peers; bind it to a material Symbolon role; and run the application and decentralization evidence on DevNet. The existing `PriceFeed` oracle is one candidate because repo and margin choices check its party identity and marks. Co-validating it could demonstrate continued availability after a host outage. Shared control would instead govern price or risk administration and require both a below-threshold rejection and a successful threshold execution. Distributed hosting alone does not prove shared control, and governance alone does not prove outage tolerance.

The brief accepts **co-validation, shared control, or both**; the evidence must match the chosen model. Judges ask for a real application risk, named node operators and thresholds, an end-to-end action, a failure or threshold test, reproducible setup, and an operator plan beyond the hackathon. Multiple demo parties on one participant do not prove this. BitSafe offers one-to-one deployment help and may join technically ready teams as a peer, but says capacity and operator fit determine selection; peer participation is not automatic. Its [DecMan repository](https://github.com/DLC-link/decentralization-manager) is Apache-2.0 open source. The brief does not establish that VPS hosting, peer hosting, network operations or all ancillary services are free; confirm those costs with BitSafe before relying on a zero-cost deployment.

The [hackathon technical starter](https://github.com/DLC-link/decentralization-manager/tree/hackathon/hackathon) is the reproducible **LocalNet** path. Its [README](https://github.com/DLC-link/decentralization-manager/blob/hackathon/hackathon/README.md) calls for at least 12 GB RAM and 4 CPUs allocated to Docker, about 20 GB of free disk, and Docker Compose v2.1.1 or newer. It brings up three local Canton participants, a local synchronizer, Postgres, and three DecMan instances. The starter pins LocalNet 0.6.12 and DecMan v1.8.0. Its auth-free, loopback-bound setup is for local demonstration, not a production or DevNet deployment. The [walkthrough](https://github.com/DLC-link/decentralization-manager/blob/hackathon/hackathon/WALKTHROUGH.md) demonstrates party creation, topology, governance DARs, and a 2-of-3 vote. That generic vote has no Symbolon application effect by itself; a Gold demo must make the decentralized party control an actual Symbolon operation and execute it on DevNet or MainNet.

The team's existing VPS is a possible **own participant** host, but it already runs other workloads. Its capacity must be checked against the participant, DecMan, database, storage-growth and peer/network requirements before deployment. The multi-participant LocalNet starter should run in a separate environment with sufficient headroom. No existing VPS service should be displaced merely to fit the starter. The [DecMan deployment guide](https://github.com/DLC-link/decentralization-manager/blob/main/docs/DEPLOYMENT_GUIDE.md) assumes an already-running Canton participant and access to its Admin and Ledger APIs; obtaining the VPS alone does not satisfy Gold.

Record the tested DecMan release instead of treating “latest” as a reproducible version. The supplied organizer messages describe DecMan as open source and mention individual assistance; this is coordination context, not an installed Symbolon dependency. cBTC is optional for this challenge: its focus is decentralization, not token choice.

## Grofty Wallet integration (deferred)

The Grofty adapter remains in the codebase as a prototype, but Grofty is outside the current challenge scope. Do not spend the remaining hackathon time on MainNet setup, wallet bounty evidence or Grofty-specific pitch claims. Its local documentation is retained as a reference if this workstream is resumed later.

The official challenge is **MainNet only**: a DevNet or TestNet integration does not qualify. Requirements include invite/manual access, a public repository, a video no longer than three minutes, and Grofty Wallet v2.0.4 or later. Integration must support the product's actual workflow rather than a disconnected wallet demonstration. MainNet operation uses real fee funding and requires applicable account/transfer readiness. Its interface rejects attempts to act or read as other parties; `prepareExecuteAndWait` is the preferred transaction path.

Symbolon now has a dedicated adapter using the official `@groftylabs/dapp-sdk@0.2.0`. It verifies the extension version and MainNet party, reads the party's own contracts, and submits the repo action builders through prepared execution. This replaces the earlier assumption that a generic PartyLayer `ledgerApi` proxy would be sufficient for Grofty. The [integration guide](../guides/grofty.md) describes the implemented boundary and verification steps.

The outstanding deployment work is substantive: Grofty account access, the core package on the actual MainNet hosting path, authorized issuer/oracle/counterparties, exact demo holdings and feed visibility, any necessary disclosure, and a reviewed fee budget. Only the core DAR's shared **DevNet** vetting has been established. That milestone does not make the MainNet workflow available.

An optional bridge direction would need a real implemented and demonstrated inbound flow. Linking to Grofty's wallet or describing its existing bridge is insufficient evidence of a Symbolon bridge integration. Funding a real CC/USDCx balance also does not create the demo CUSD/CETH consumed by the current repo contracts.

No MainNet transaction, fee payment, native bridge result or bounty eligibility is claimed. Tests using the actual SDK with a fake provider establish client behavior only. The final demonstration must clearly disclose simulated assets and show the core product action that actually committed on the required network.
